100 days off: what the data say

By day 100 the direction is set. The difference between keeping it and losing it was made in the first 30 days.

2026-11-08

Day 100 is not magic. It is about fourteen half-lives: the drug fully gone, the body settled into its new balance, two more months on top. Where the 7-day average sits then says most of what a year will say.

Two paths

In trials, regain a year after stopping was about two-thirds of the loss. In real-world data (Cleveland Clinic, 2026), average regain a year later was far smaller. Same drug, same stop. The difference: people in real clinics did something in the first few months. Rebuilt a routine, or went to a maintenance dose with their clinician.

So the day-100 result was not decided on day 100. It was decided in the first 30 days.

What to look at on day 100

  • 7-day average within 20% of the lowest: your routine is the answer. Do not change it.
  • Holding between 20 and 40%: that is stability. It may be your new baseline.
  • Past 40% and still rising: routine alone is not enough. Time to discuss a maintenance dose.

The next 100 days

The second hundred is quieter. Daily logging matters less; two or three weigh-ins a week keep the 7-day average alive. The one thing not to miss is appetite rising two weeks in a row. Only then go back to daily.
