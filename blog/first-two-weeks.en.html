The first two weeks after stopping a GLP-1 medicine

The medicine does not leave at once. Because of its half-life, some is still on board in the first two weeks.

2026-09-13

After the last dose it is easy to expect appetite to return straight away. The first week is often quieter than that, so logging slips and changes are noticed only weeks later.

The medicine does not leave at once

According to the prescribing information, semaglutide has a half-life of about one week and tirzepatide about five days. With a one-week half-life, about half is left a week after the last dose and roughly a quarter after two weeks. That is one reason the first fortnight can feel quiet.

What studies saw after stopping

Weight coming back after stopping has been seen in many studies. In the extension of a semaglutide trial, about two-thirds of the weight lost had returned a year after stopping. A 2026 meta-analysis of 37 studies found weight rose by an average of 0.4 kg a month after stopping, and 0.8 kg a month in the semaglutide and tirzepatide studies.

Averages are averages. In real-world data from Cleveland Clinic, average regain one year later was 0.5% of body weight, which the researchers linked to many people continuing another treatment or lifestyle care with their clinician. Individual courses differ.

What to do with the quiet fortnight

It is a good time to build a baseline: one weight under the same conditions each day, plus appetite and food-noise scores. Ten seconds. With a baseline, later weeks can be compared side by side.
