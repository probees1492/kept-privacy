The first two weeks off Wegovy: what actually happens

The drug does not leave at once. With a one-week half-life, the first two weeks are still medicated time.

2026-09-13

After the last injection most people expect appetite to explode. Instead the first week is strangely quiet. Portions stay the same, the scale stays the same. So you relax, stop logging, and in week three you get a surprise.

The drug does not leave at once

Semaglutide (Wegovy) has a half-life of about seven days. A week after the last dose half is still there; after two weeks, a quarter. Tirzepatide (Mounjaro, Zepbound) clears a little faster at about five days. Either way, the first two weeks are still medicated time.

That is why nothing moves. Your body is not holding the line; the drug is still working.

The real change starts in week three

From week three the order is predictable.

  1. Food thoughts come back (food noise)
  2. Portions creep up
  3. Two to three weeks later the 7-day average weight rises

In the STEP-1 extension, people who stopped semaglutide regained about two-thirds of their lost weight within a year, and most of that regain was front-loaded. Real-world data from 2026 show a very different outcome for people who noticed early and acted.

The quiet fortnight is for the baseline

To make these two weeks count, do one thing: one weight and two sliders (appetite, food noise) every day. Ten seconds.

That baseline is what lets you see week three before the scale does. Daily weight swings a kilogram with water and salt; a 7-day average and an appetite score do not.
