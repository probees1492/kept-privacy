Why Kept uses a 7-day average, not the scale

A single morning weight swings about a kilogram. That is why the status waits a few days.

2026-09-27

You step on the scale and it is up 0.8 kg from yesterday. You remember the salty dinner and the extra water, and the mood still sinks for the day. Tomorrow it is down 0.6 and you relax. Repeat for a month and you will hate the scale.

A day's weight is mostly water

Five grams of salt holds a litre of water. A hundred grams of carbohydrate stores 300 to 400 g of water with glycogen. Digestion, the menstrual cycle and short sleep each move hundreds of grams. Real fat change, about 100 g a day, is buried under that noise.

That is why trials report weekly averages or fixed-day weights. A single day is noise, not data.

What a 7-day moving average does

Average the last seven days and the salt and water swings cancel out. What is left is direction. How far that average sits above your lowest weight is the one trustworthy number for judging regain.

So for the first few days there is no verdict to give, because there is no average yet. The job in that window is logging, not judging.

Two rules

  1. Same conditions every time: morning, after the bathroom, no clothes.
  2. Look at the daily number if you like, but judge only by the 7-day average and its direction.

In Kept

The big number on Today is not today's weight; it is how far the 7-day average has come back from your lowest. The status (holding steady, worth watching, talk to your doctor) is set only from that average. Until a few days are in, it says "building your baseline". That is the honest display.
