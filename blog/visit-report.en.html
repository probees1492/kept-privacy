How to use a one-page report in a five-minute appointment

Follow-ups are short. Hand over one page instead of describing, and the conversation changes.

2026-11-01

Follow-ups are short. "How has it been?" "Gained a bit." "Appetite?" "Up a little." There is nothing in that exchange for a clinician to work with, so most end in "let's hold for now".

What the clinician actually wants to see

  1. Lowest weight, current 7-day average, and the gap as a share of the loss
  2. When appetite and food noise started climbing
  3. When the last dose was and which doses came before
  4. Any side effects, and at which dose

With those four on one page the clinician reads the situation in thirty seconds and spends the remaining four and a half minutes on the decision: maintenance dose, wider interval, or more time on routine.

One page, not many

Nobody reads several pages, and nobody wants to swipe through an app. One sheet of paper or one screen, readable top to bottom in thirty seconds: one chart, four numbers, a dose table.

The evening before

Make the report and save it to Photos or Files; it is faster than opening an app in the room. Then decide on one question, such as "what about a maintenance dose?" The report is the evidence for that question.
