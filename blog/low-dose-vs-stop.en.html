Lower dose vs. stopping: what the trials say

People who stayed on a reduced dose kept more of the loss than people who stopped outright.

2026-10-04

"I cannot afford to keep going, and if I stop it all comes back." That sentence is missing an option: taking less.

Which way the research points

In STEP-4, the group switched from semaglutide to placebo regained about two-thirds of the loss within a year. SURMOUNT-4 showed the same for tirzepatide. Maintenance studies from 2025 and 2026 looked at a different group: people kept on half the dose, or a dose every two weeks. Their regain stopped at less than half of the full-stop group's.

The reason is simple. Keeping the appetite circuits quiet takes less drug than losing weight did.

In money terms

A month of Wegovy in 2026 runs from the low hundreds at the starting dose to several hundred at the top dose, depending on market and coverage. A half dose or a two-week interval cuts that by half or more. The realistic choice is not all-or-nothing; it is a fraction of the cost for most of the maintenance effect.

This is a decision with your clinician

Changing dose or interval is a prescription. This article only says the option exists; which dose fits you is your clinician's call. One question is enough at the appointment: "Instead of stopping completely, what about a maintenance dose?"
